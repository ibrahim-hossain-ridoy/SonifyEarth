Section 2 · The Mission & Orbital Observation Problem

Why Orbital Lightning Silence Has Two Completely Different Meanings

Unlike a geostationary weather satellite staring at one hemisphere, the International Space Station races around Earth at 400 km altitude along a 51.6° inclined orbit. Its Lightning Imaging Sensor (LIS) sweeps a narrow ~550 km × 550 km optical camera footprint, viewing any single storm cell for only ~80–95 seconds per overpass.

Scientific Innovation

From Binary Event Maps to Three-State Epistemic Honesty

Conventional lightning sonifications and maps plot only flash dots on a blank background—treating unviewed latitudes (> ±54°), South Atlantic Anomaly radiation zones (18,571 s across 47 transits), and telemetry outages (8,232 s fatal flags + 5,519.2 s Nov 11 QC gap) identically to a genuinely calm sky.

  • Swath Geometry Reality: Out of 259,200 global 0.5° × 0.5° cells on Earth, ISS LIS observed 160,560 cells during Nov 9–16, 2023, while 98,640 cells poleward of [-55.75°, +55.25°] received zero viewtime.
  • Diurnal Sensitivity Context: Because sunlight reflects off cloud tops, ISS LIS optical detection efficiency ranges from ~69% near local solar noon to ~88% at night (science_v2_guide.pdf p. 20).
  • Optical Radiance ≠ Storm Severity: Flash radiance (uJ/sr/m2/um) measures cloud-top scattered light, which depends on cloud optical depth and viewing angle—so SonifyEarth keeps flash pulse loudness uniform and never labels radiance as storm destructiveness.

Stage 1: NASA ISS Lightning Imaging Sensor (LIS) Instrument

Inspect Live Stage Evidence ↓

High-speed 128×128 pixel Charge-Coupled Device (CCD) optical imaging array aboard the International Space Station detecting narrow-band 777.4 nm atomic oxygen lightning emissions at ~2 ms frame intervals (~500 frames/s).

1. Stage Input

Optical cloud-top photon emissions at 777.4 nm + spacecraft GPS/attitude ephemeris at ~400 km altitude (51.6° inclination).

2. Information Contained

~550 km × 550 km field of view (~4 km nadir pixel resolution), viewing any ground cell for ~80–95 s per orbit across [-55.75°, +55.25°] latitude.

3. Processing Applied

Real-time background subtraction removes bright sunlit cloud tops to isolate millisecond optical lightning transients (~69% noon to ~88% night efficiency).

4. Why It Matters Scientifically

Because the camera moves at 7.66 km/s, it only observes a tiny fraction of Earth at any moment—establishing why unviewed areas must never be called "zero lightning."

5. Output Produced

Bitmask Quality Control (0x55 Fatal vs. 0xAA Warning)

  • Fatal Odd-Bit Mask (0x55 = 0b01010101): Bit 0 (0x01 instrument fatal), Bit 2 (0x04 platform fatal), Bit 4 (0x10 external fatal), Bit 6 (0x40 processing fatal). Any record with (alert_flag & 0x55) != 0 is excluded from DETECTED/OBSERVED_NO_DETECTION and classified UNKNOWN (FATAL_QUALITY_FLAG).
  • Warning Even-Bit Mask (0xAA = 0b10101010): Informational warnings only. Records with (alert_flag & 0x55) == 0 remain scientifically valid.
  • 105 Fatal-Flagged Raw Flashes (Class 1 vs. Contiguous): Out of 16,124 raw flashes, 105 carry fatal flags (84 fatal-only outside SAA + 21 fatal+SAA). Exactly 100 of these 105 occur during isolated 1-second framing/bit-toggle events (Class 1 anomaly), while 5 occur inside multi-minute contiguous fatal telemetry outages (Class 2 anomaly).
  • 10 Contiguous Fatal Outage Windows (8,232 s Total): Includes 9 platform/instrument outage runs across 9 granules plus the major 4,818.0 s (80.3 min) outage in ISS_LIS_SC_V2.2_20231116_172457_FIN.nc.

1. Leap-Second-Aware TAI93 to UTC Conversion

NASA ISS LIS timestamps are elapsed International Atomic Time (TAI) seconds since 1993-01-01T00:00:00Z (when TAI − UTC = 27.0 s). Between January 1993 and November 2023 (when TAI − UTC = 37.0 s, last leap second added 2016-12-31), 10.0 leap seconds accumulated:

UTC = Epoch(1993-01-01T00:00:00Z) + (TAI93 − 10.0 s)

Validated across all 106 Science and Backgrounds granules against official NASA ISO-8601 global attributes with 0.000000 s discrepancy (TAI93_UTC_VALIDATION_REPORT.md).

2. Spatial Point-in-Cell Floor Matching

NASA ISS LIS viewtime grid centers sit at half-degree midpoints (-89.75° … +89.75° lat, -179.75° … +179.75° lon). Mapping flash coordinates via standard floor binning:

cell_center = floor(coord × 2) / 2 + 0.25°

Matches 100.00% (16,124 / 16,124) of flashes within <= 0.5° of an active viewtime cell in the same granule (`99.78%` exact point-in-cell + `0.22%` [36 flashes] adjacent optical footprint spillover).

3. Five Explicit UNKNOWN Reason Codes

  • INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_SAA_RADIATION: Trapped proton radiation in South Atlantic Anomaly (47 windows, 18,571 s).
  • FATAL_QUALITY_FLAG: Odd-bit fatal instrument/platform/external/processing mask ((flag & 0x55) != 0).
  • MISSING_SOURCE_DATA: Nov 11, 2023 NASA QC deleted-orbit gap (5,519.2 s) or post-decommissioning tail (2,795.2 s).
  • OUTSIDE_OBSERVATION_COVERAGE: Locations outside the active ~550 km swath or poleward of ±54° (98,640 unviewed polar cells).
  • UNRESOLVED_TIME_MATCH: Fallback code for any timestamp outside validated granule bounds (0 unresolved in production).
